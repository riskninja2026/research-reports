THE THIRTY-SECOND VERSION

If you read only one screen, read this one

  • Who: NVIDIA designs the chips and systems that train and run AI. It owns no factories — contract manufacturers build the chips — and its CUDA software keeps customers on the platform; Data Center is 92.5% of revenue.
  • This period: quarterly revenue reached US$96.2bn, more than double a year ago, and about 62 cents of every revenue dollar became net profit — but only 40% of that profit arrived as operating cash.
  • The tension: NVIDIA increasingly helps finance its own demand: selected investment-grade customers get 90 days up to a year to pay, it buys cloud capacity and equity stakes in its ecosystem, and its guarantees can reach US$108.5bn. Demand is real — but NVIDIA now shares its customers' credit risk.
  • The trap: the headline 26× trailing P/E (price-to-earnings ratio) is struck at the regular close on earnings holding US$30.6bn of trailing investment gains; at the after-hours price the raw multiple is 27.5×, and about 32× with those gains stripped. The outlook also assumes zero China Data Center compute revenue, so recovery there is upside, not the base case.
  • Verdict: Neutral-cautious (watch): the business is exceptional; the odds are not — the base case is worth about US$254 a share against a US$219.53 price, and the downside to the US$106 bear case is larger than the upside. It improves if margin holds the promised floor and guarantees shrink through use.
PRIMER

Designs the AI chips, outsources the factories, and now helps finance the buyers

NVIDIA designs GPUs — processors built to do huge numbers of calculations in parallel, exactly what training and running AI models requires. It is a fabless company: it designs the chips and systems, and contract manufacturers fabricate them. Its customers are mostly the giant cloud companies (hyperscalers), AI-cloud providers, enterprises and governments building AI capacity.

Step 1 ·
Design the platform

Chips, networking and whole systems, refreshed yearly: Blackwell today, Vera Rubin next.

Step 2 ·
Sell to the AI builders

Cloud giants and AI companies buy systems by the tens of billions; one direct customer alone was 16% of quarterly revenue.

Step 3 ·
CUDA keeps them

The software layer NVIDIA chips are programmed through; leaving means rebuilding performance and reliability elsewhere.

Step 4 ·
Now: finance the buyers

Longer payment terms, equity stakes, cloud purchases and guarantees behind customers' build-outs — the step this report scrutinizes.

Three reading aids. First, the calendar: NVIDIA's fiscal year runs ahead of the calendar — FY2027 is roughly calendar 2026, this report covers the quarter ended 26 July 2026, and the fourth quarter of FY2027 contains an extra week. Second, the product map: "Data Center", the AI-chip business, is 92.5% of revenue; the two audited segments are Compute & Networking and Graphics, while "market platforms" are a management sales taxonomy. Third, the numbers to trust: figures come from SEC filings, the quarter is unaudited under U.S. GAAP, and the call and broker research supply context only. Terms like CUDA carry a dotted underline and are defined in the glossary.

Why this quarter matters: the AI build-out is entering its capital-heavy phase, some of NVIDIA's biggest customers are spending more on data centers than they generate in cash, and NVIDIA has started standing behind some of that spending. The question is not whether AI demand is real, but how much of NVIDIA's future demand depends on its own balance sheet.

PLAIN ENGLISH

This quarter's revenue (US$96.2bn) is more than three and a half times NVIDIA's entire FY2022 year. The margins belong to software, not hardware: 75 cents of gross profit per revenue dollar, 62 of net profit.

The blemish is the cash column: over full years NVIDIA converts most of its profit to cash (87.9% of net income in FY2025 and 85.6% in FY2026), but this quarter operating cash flow (US$24.1bn) was only about 40% of net income (US$59.7bn), because customers may pay later and inventory is stockpiled before Rubin. Both move risk onto NVIDIA's own books, the thread this report pulls.

Company FCF is NVIDIA's own measure: operating cash flow less purchases of, and principal payments on, property, equipment and intangibles; volumes and selling prices are not disclosed.

PRODUCT · INDUSTRY

Demand is real and increasingly financed; Rubin must land without a write-down

The near-term test is the handoff from Blackwell to the next generation, Vera Rubin. The filings reveal no product-level yields or margins, so investors can only watch for the absence of bad news: no inventory write-down, no deeper drop in group gross margin.

Blackwell (incl. Ultra)

The architecture is the majority of system shipments and the vast majority of revenue; the Ultra refresh drove the sequential Data Center ramp, but its own share, volume, price and utilization are undisclosed.

Vera Rubin

Production shipments began in Q3 FY2027 and management targets a material next-quarter contribution; yield, memory configuration, mix and margin unproven.

Vera CPU (call only)

Management described about US$20bn of server-CPU demand and preliminarily expects CPU revenue to more than double in FY2028; realized revenue and margin unproven.

Networking

Another record quarter on the call, Spectrum-X Ethernet up 2.6× year on year; the filing discloses no networking dollars, so profitability is unknown.

Research spending is expensed as incurred, so reported profit already absorbs it: the quarter's outlay rose 64% from a year ago, driven by a 127% jump in compute infrastructure and 30% higher pay, and 31,000 of roughly 42,000 employees worked in research at FY2026 year-end. The filings cannot show the return on any architecture; the test is whether each yearly generation raises customers' useful output faster than memory, power and software costs rise. CUDA is where that test is fought, workload by workload.

Memory moves the margin trough

Management told investors on the call that gross margin should bottom at 71%–72% in Q4 FY2027 and settle at 72%–73% in FY2028 after price increases. The pressure is real: Samsung and SK hynix both describe tight server-memory supply and the ramp of HBM4, the high-bandwidth memory NVIDIA's systems depend on. A floor promised is not a floor delivered; the claim fails if margin breaks the trough or the price increases dent demand.

Demand is strong, but the buyers are financing it

The biggest customers' own filings back the demand story and show the strain: cloud revenue is accelerating while free cash flow falls at some buyers as they borrow to build. Booming demand and stretched buyers can both be true.

China is excluded from the base case

China has effectively dropped out of the numbers. Licensed H200 shipments were under 1% of quarterly Data Center revenue; the first half absorbed a US$0.4 billion charge for H200 inventory and purchase obligations; licensed units carry a 25% tariff NVIDIA says it cannot pass on. The next-quarter outlook assumes zero China Data Center compute revenue. NVIDIA warns that being locked out helps rival hardware and developer ecosystems grow — upside if licensing improves, an incubator of competition until then.

PLAIN ENGLISH

Follow the money in the buyer table: Alphabet's cloud is growing 82% but its free cash flow went negative, Amazon's trailing-year free cash flow is negative too, and Microsoft added US$115.9bn of plant in a year. That does not make the demand fake — it means part of the demand is financed, and financed demand is more fragile in a downturn.

EARNINGS QUALITY

Operating leverage is real; GAAP income overstates the run rate

The core machine got stronger, but the bottom line got help from below the operating line. Gains on equity investments of US$7.771bn equaled 10.87% of quarterly pretax income; across the first half they reached US$23.707bn, or 16.76% of pretax income, and US$30.6bn over the last twelve months — the amount the valuation strips. Those equity-security gains come from NVIDIA's investment portfolio riding the AI boom, not from selling chips, and they can reverse.

A definitional change helps: from Q1 FY2027, NVIDIA's own non-GAAP profit measure stopped adding back stock-based compensation — employee pay in shares is now counted as the real cost it is. The remaining gap between GAAP and adjusted profit is now driven visibly by investment gains, deal costs and tax effects.

Positive interpretation

Gross margin held near three-quarters while operating expense was only 8.7% of revenue. The platform is converting exceptional growth into operating leverage.

Necessary adjustment

The raw trailing P/E uses earnings inflated by security gains. On the after-hours price the raw multiple is 27.5×; the filing-derived, gain-adjusted proxy is roughly 31.6–32.7×.

PLAIN ENGLISH

When you read that NVIDIA trades at 26 times earnings, that figure is struck at the regular close and on earnings that include investment windfalls. On the after-hours price the same raw multiple is 27.5×, and stripping the windfalls takes it to about 32×: the headline understates what you are paying for the chip business. That distinction runs through the valuation section.

BALANCE SHEET · CREDIT

NVIDIA is becoming a credit allocator

Even as profits soared, less of them arrived as cash, because NVIDIA is extending credit to customers and building inventory. Accounts receivable — money customers owe but have not paid — grew faster than revenue after selected investment-grade customers were given terms of 90 days up to one year for large multi-quarter builds. Days sales outstanding (DSO) jumped from 45 to 60 in a single quarter, and the balance sits with the five direct customers the financial map counts. Inventory rose ahead of the Rubin launch. Neither proves demand is weak; both shift timing and counterparty risk onto NVIDIA.

Liquidity and the investment book

Liquid resources are ample but not the whole story. Cash and marketable debt securities of US$56.586bn against US$33.366bn of debt leave US$23.220bn of net cash, which the valuation adds once. Around it sits a US$98.897bn equity-investment footprint — marketable and non-marketable stakes plus equity-method investments — that is not operating cash, is valued partly on sparse private transactions, and is haircut by scenario.

The largest piece: after quarter-end NVIDIA signed a guarantee of up to US$105 billion supporting SB Energy data-center sites whose capacity OpenAI will use. It phases in across nine sites as each becomes ready — the first expected in FY2029 — shrinks as the tenant pays and can end early under specified credit conditions; NVIDIA also holds an option to extend credit support to about 3.8 more gigawatts, and the smaller existing pool has US$0.712 billion of escrow behind it. If a supported customer defaults, NVIDIA may have to pay.

Separately, NVIDIA talks about helping mobilize more than US$500 billion of third-party infrastructure capital, resting on preliminary, nonbinding memoranda — the capital providers underwrite each project, though NVIDIA may at its option provide limited residual-value support on specific ones — so it is a financing funnel, not funded capacity, and not an exposure-free one. Management rejects the credit framing outright: on the call the CFO said NVIDIA is "not making loans". The cash question went unanswered even so: asked what the commitments require in FY2028 cash, the CEO defended platform durability and the CFO said the supply commitments are concentrated in the first three years. No return hurdle or loss probability was disclosed — and none was asked for.

PLAIN ENGLISH

NVIDIA now does two jobs: selling chips, and financing the people who buy them. Customers take longer to pay (days to collect a sale went from 45 to 60 in one quarter; five customers owe roughly US$44bn), and the perimeter above runs to US$366bn of scheduled commitments, US$56bn of additional agreements and US$108.5bn of maximum guarantees.

Three cautions. They are gross ceilings, not expected losses — loss probabilities are undisclosed. The filing provides no overlap reconciliation, so they cannot be added into one total. And the biggest guarantee does not start until FY2029. Management did not answer the cash question, so shareholders are underwriting a credit book with no published credit policy.

CAPITAL · GOVERNANCE

Buybacks offset dilution; investments change the risk budget

Over the cycle NVIDIA gives capital back: from FY2022 through the first half of FY2027, buybacks and dividends totaled about US$141.7 billion against roughly US$29.1 billion raised from employee stock plans and net new debt — 4.9 dollars returned per dollar raised. The mix is shifting: this half-year more cash went into equity stakes than into buybacks.

On dilution: this quarter's buybacks were 2.94× the gross shares issued to employees, and the share count fell from 24.221 billion to 24.147 billion, a net drop of 0.31%. One quarter of offset says nothing about future years.

Equity pay is performance-linked, but the only scored year paid 150–200% of target

No stock options have been outstanding since FY2024; the live economics are restricted and performance stock units. The Fiscal 2026 single-year hurdle was non-GAAP operating income of US$46bn, US$71bn and US$96bn at threshold, target and stretch — and that stretch had been cut automatically from US$120bn when the H20 export controls landed, then cleared with US$137.3bn. That paid 200% of target to the named executives other than the CEO and 150% to the CEO, whose award is capped there because he receives performance units only. The multi-year hurdle is three-year relative shareholder return, on the same split; the proxy sets no FY2027 goal. The tail is long: US$19.4bn of unearned stock pay will be expensed over about 2.6 years. One weakness in the FY2026 design: that single-year metric added SBC back, weakening discipline over the equity-pay cost — non-GAAP stopped excluding SBC only from Q1 FY2027.

Founder influence is economic, not majority control

Founder-CEO Jen-Hsun Huang held 3.58% of the company at the March proxy date; all directors and officers together held 3.94%. Nine of the ten directors were independent, with an independent lead director, and a July appointment expanded the board to eleven. NVIDIA still discloses no Chief Compliance Officer, as export controls and customer financing grow more consequential. Two competition matters are open. In September 2025 China's antitrust authorities published a preliminary finding that NVIDIA's export-compliant degraded products discriminated unfairly against Chinese customers and violated the Mellanox approval terms. The French competition authority is separately asking whether gaming and data-center GPUs are distinct products. Neither an exposure nor a reserve is disclosed for either.

PLAIN ENGLISH

The shareholder-friendly facts: buybacks and dividends have returned about 4.9× what stock plans and new debt brought in; executive awards carry profit and share-price hurdles, though the Fiscal 2026 profit goal was cut with the export controls and then paid out at 200% of target for the other named executives and 150% for the CEO, capped there because his equity is performance-only. The watch-list: US$19.4bn of granted stock pay will hit expenses over the coming years; China is at a preliminary adverse finding with no disclosed maximum penalty; and there is no disclosed Chief Compliance Officer while export-control and customer-financing exposures grow.

VALUATION APPENDIX

How the load-bearing numbers were built

Owner FCFF = EBIT × (1 − tax) + D&A (depreciation and amortization) − capex − working-capital absorption, with no SBC add-back. The DCF discounts FY2028–FY2030 at the scenario weighted average cost of capital (WACC), adds a terminal value of FY2030 FCFF × (1 + g) ÷ (WACC − g), adds net cash once and the scenario share of the equity holdings, subtracts the capital-risk haircut and divides by 24.285 billion diluted shares. Its rate is anchored on NVIDIA's own funding cost, the 4.95% coupon on the 2036 notes issued in June 2026. The cross-check strips security gains and keeps a normalized other-income line of 0.2% / 0.4% / 0.5% of revenue by scenario — an analyst stand-in for interest on the retained cash, so net cash is not added twice. It then applies the scenario multiple to FY2029 normalized net income, discounts two years, adds the recognized equity holdings less the haircut, and divides by the same 24.285 billion diluted count as the DCF. The scenario share path — that count 1.5% higher in Bear where issuance outruns buybacks, 24.30bn in Base (effectively flat), 1.4% lower in Bull — sets only the FY2029 EPS in the scenario table.

RISKS

The operating thesis is intact; the evidentiary burden is higher

Against it: part of future demand now depends on NVIDIA's own investments, terms, leases and guarantees, whose returns and loss probabilities are undisclosed.

PLAIN ENGLISH

Rank one decides the thesis: whether the financing promises stay promises. The US$366bn of scheduled commitments, US$56bn of additional agreements and US$108.5bn of maximum guarantees are ceilings, not expected losses — and even the bear case charges only US$85.0bn of them, about US$3.50 a share. So the damage would arrive through the margin trough and slower growth long before a guarantee is called. What settles it is use, not talk: commitments running off as capacity is used, no assumed leases, receivables collected on time.

DECISION

Neutral-cautious (watch): an exceptional business at odds that are not yet generous

NVIDIA owns the strongest operating evidence in AI infrastructure, yet on this report's own model the downside to the bear case is wider than the upside to the base case, and the credit economics that would size that downside — hurdle rates, loss probabilities — are nowhere disclosed.

ThesisThe platform moat keeps widening while NVIDIA's own balance sheet underwrites a growing share of demand; the price discounts execution risk, not a demand collapse, and shareholders carry a credit book with no published credit policy.
Base vs consensusNo usable post-results consensus. Wind's snapshot (queried 2026-08-26) carries no vintage and is stale against the first half plus the third-quarter guide. The implied-expectations table stands in its place: the price asks for FY2028 growth of about 46.5% against the Base case. The one dated post-call broker mark is Goldman Sachs's US$300 target. It sits inside the Base interval, but it shares the normalized-earnings logic.
OddsOwner-FCFF DCF against the after-hours price, on the model's own three-year horizon and with carry included in each: Bear −46.0% versus Base +21.6%, Bull +157.1%. The cross-check method is not mixed in.
Break-even priceUS$191.04 — the carry-adjusted price at which base upside equals bear downside over the same three years; the market sits 14.9% above it.
CarryAbout 1.9% a year, 5.9% compounded over the model's three-year horizon: the dividend at its current US$0.25 quarterly rate (set 18 May 2026) is 0.46% of the after-hours price, plus a buyback yield of roughly 1.47% from annualizing H1 FY2027 repurchases against the US$5.301tn after-hours market value.
Confidence by pillarThesis evidence — High: the commitment, guarantee and receivable disclosures are filed, not inferred. Cash and balance sheet — Medium: quarterly cash conversion and undisclosed credit economics. Governance — Medium: no Chief Compliance Officer, open antitrust matters. Lowest pillar, valuation inputs — Low: FY2028 growth rests on a call-only outlook and the terminal margin does most of the work; the observable that would move it is a dated FY2028 revenue framing in the Q4 FY2027 guidance.
VerdictNeutral-cautious (watch). Bear downside exceeds base upside even after carry; the band improves if gross margin holds the promised floor and guarantees shrink through use rather than convert into assumed leases and payments. Next checkpoint: the Q3 FY2027 results — Rubin mix, receivable aging, commitment run-off.
Strongest counterargument: the preliminary FY2028 outlook is limited by supply, not demand, and the balance-sheet structures may end up a profitable second business with few realized losses — in which case the moat is widening and the balance sheet is underwriting it profitably. Failure boundary: customer-financed demand, weaker cash conversion and lower margins persisting together would invalidate the premium platform valuation.

The terms you need for an AI-infrastructure company

TermPlain-English meaning
Graphics processing unit (GPU)A processor that runs huge numbers of calculations in parallel — the hardware AI models train and run on.
FablessA chip company that designs chips but owns no factories; contract manufacturers fabricate them.
HyperscalerA giant cloud-computing company that buys hardware at enormous scale.
Data Center · Hyperscale · AI Clouds, Industrial & Enterprise (ACIE)NVIDIA's labels for its AI-computing business, 92.5% of revenue: Hyperscale is the cloud giants; ACIE is AI clouds, enterprises, industry and governments.
CUDA software platformNVIDIA's software layer; code and skills built on it are costly to move to rival hardware — the moat.
Blackwell / Vera RubinThe current and next chip generations; the architecture now changes roughly every year.
High-bandwidth memory (HBM4)Memory stacked next to AI chips; scarce, expensive, and the main squeeze on margins.
After-hours indicationA price quoted after the regular session closes; indicative until the next session settles it — this report's US$219.53.
Segment (reportable)A business unit reported separately in the audited accounts: Compute & Networking and Graphics.
Stock-based compensation (SBC)Paying employees in shares — a real cost to owners through dilution even though no cash leaves.
Non-GAAP profitA company's own adjusted profit measure; NVIDIA's now keeps SBC as a cost and strips investment gains.
Equity-security gainsPaper and realized gains on shares NVIDIA owns in other companies — investment results, not chip profits.
Company FCF vs owner FCFFCompany FCF is NVIDIA's own measure: operating cash flow minus capital spending and the principal paid on it. Owner FCFF, this report's stricter measure, also charges stock pay and the cash absorbed by receivables and inventory.
Working capital (NWC)Cash tied up in running the business — customer IOUs and inventory minus supplier credit; when it grows, profit outruns cash.
Accounts receivable / days sales outstanding (DSO)Money customers owe / the average days to collect it; NVIDIA's DSO jumped from 45 to 60 days.
Guarantee (contingent)A promise to cover someone else's obligations if they fail — a ceiling of possible loss, not debt.
Weighted average cost of capital (WACC)The yearly return investors demand for the risk; the DCF's discount rate.
Terminal growthThe growth rate assumed forever after the forecast years; with the discount rate it sets most of a DCF's value — here 2.5% to 4.5% by scenario.
CarryWhat you collect while you wait — dividends plus buybacks, about 1.9% a year here — added to both the upside and the downside so the odds cover the same three years.
Restricted / performance stock units (RSU / PSU)RSUs vest with time; PSUs need profit or share-price hurdles; the CEO receives PSUs only.
