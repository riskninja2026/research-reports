The moat is widening.
The balance sheet is underwriting it.

NVIDIA's second quarter confirms extraordinary demand, platform breadth and a fast Vera Rubin handoff. But the company is no longer only selling compute: it is also reserving supply, buying cloud capacity, extending customer terms and guaranteeing infrastructure. The equity now requires underwriting both a technology monopoly and an increasingly consequential credit-allocation engine.

R&D is expensed; infrastructure is the fastest-growing input

Quarterly R&D expense reached US$7.054 billion, up 64% year on year. Compute-infrastructure expense increased 127% and compensation increased 30%. The filing therefore shows real economic spend rather than capitalized internal R&D, but it does not disclose architecture-level return on investment. The better productivity test is whether annual cadence raises useful-token output, networking attach and customer utilization faster than memory, power and software costs.

At FY2026 year-end, 31,000 of roughly 42,000 employees worked in R&D. NVIDIA reports numerous issued and pending patents with expiries from March 2026 through June 2045, but no patent count or patent-level revenue. Protection comes from patents, trade secrets and licenses together. The non-exclusive Groq technology license is the clearest return-risk example: its US$2.5 billion developed-technology asset must be integrated into commercial products, while the larger goodwill component cannot itself prove a moat.

PLAIN ENGLISH

CUDA is not just an API. The defensible layer is the production lifecycle around kernels, inference engines, memory management, routing, collectives, networking and system integration. Porting code can be easier than reproducing performance, reliability and utilization. That advantage must still be re-earned workload by workload against custom accelerators and rival merchant silicon.

Memory moves the margin trough

The call says gross margin should bottom at 71%–72% in Q4 FY2027 and settle at 72%–73% in FY2028 after pricing actions. Samsung and SK hynix independently describe HBM4 commercialization and tight server-memory supply, which supports the direction of the constraint. Goldman treats the bounded trough as a potential clearing event for HBM-cost fears. That is an expectations reset, not evidence that Rubin pricing or economics have been realized. Failure means either margin misses the trough or price increases weaken demand.

INDUSTRY · CHINA · OVERSEAS

Demand is strong, but the buyers are financing it

Official buyer disclosures corroborate the demand side of NVIDIA's call: cloud revenue is accelerating and AI infrastructure spending remains exceptional. They also show the counterweight. Free cash flow is falling at some buyers, debt and equity funding are rising, and customer-designed accelerators remain a workload-specific alternative. Strong compute demand and financing strain can coexist.

China is excluded from the base case

H200 shipments under U.S. licenses were less than 1% of quarterly Data Center revenue. First-half results include a US$0.4 billion H200 inventory and purchase-obligation charge. Licensed units must pass through a U.S. inspection and incur a 25% tariff that NVIDIA says it cannot pass to customers. The next-quarter outlook includes no China Data Center compute revenue.

The loss is not only near-term sales. NVIDIA says effective foreclosure helps rival hardware and developer ecosystems, including deployments of globally relevant open models on competing platforms. China therefore remains scenario upside, not base-case revenue, and may become a source of global competition even if licensing later improves.

Reported geography is a billing map, not consumption

Customers headquartered outside the United States represented 38% of Q2 revenue. NVIDIA explicitly warns that direct-customer headquarters can differ from end customer and shipment location. The same limitation applies to concentration: one direct customer was 16% of Q2 revenue, but a separately described AI research company may buy cloud services through several direct and indirect channels.

Currency translation is not the load-bearing overseas risk. Substantially all sales are denominated in U.S. dollars, while forward contracts hedge mainly operating expenses and monetary balances; first-half contract gains, losses and fair values were not significant. Nearly all cash held abroad can be used in the United States without additional federal tax, except approximately US$1.7 billion that could incur foreign or state tax on repatriation. Export licensing and offshore manufacturing therefore matter more than reported foreign-exchange noise.

Founder influence is economic, not majority control

The March proxy reports Jen-Hsun Huang with 3.58% beneficial ownership and all directors and executive officers with 3.94%. Huang's figure includes foundation shares in which the proxy says he has no pecuniary interest. NVIDIA had nine independent directors and Huang at the proxy date, with an independent lead director; a July appointment later expanded the board. The structure gives the founder strategic influence without voting control.

External-holder dates are not aligned. Vanguard's proxy-reproduced position was 7.31% at the end of March 2026; BlackRock's 7.43% figure rests on a split-adjusted holding dated December 2023 and is not a current register. The dates matter more than the apparent ranking.

Board risk oversight explicitly covers business-model, supply-chain, investment and regulatory risks. NVIDIA nevertheless discloses no Chief Compliance Officer, while export controls and customer financing have become more material. Related-person transactions require Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee approval. The proxy also discloses employment of Huang's two adult children under comparable-role compensation processes; neither reports directly to him.

The Huang Foundation separately agreed to buy CoreWeave GPU compute for university and nonprofit donations, while NVIDIA planned ordinary-course free engineering services for certain recipients. The proxy does not quantify NVIDIA's service cost and does not characterize foundation spending as NVIDIA revenue, expense or donation. The relationship is a perception and conflict-monitoring item, not evidence of circular revenue.

Competition inquiries are broad; China is further advanced

NVIDIA continues to receive information requests from competition regulators in the European Union, United States, United Kingdom, China and South Korea. The requests cover GPU sales, supply allocation, foundation-model investments and partnerships, roadmaps, and customer and supplier agreements. France is also examining whether gaming and data-center GPUs are separate product categories.

China's stage is more advanced. On 15 September 2025, regulators published a preliminary finding that NVIDIA's export-control-compliant degraded products discriminated against Chinese customers and violated terms of the Mellanox approval. Potential remedies include financial penalties and restrictions or orders affecting networking products, services and operations. The filing does not separately disclose a maximum exposure or accrual for these competition matters. Closure requires a formal disposition without remedies, or a disclosed order, penalty or operating restriction.

A March 2026 court order certified a shareholder class concerning alleged 2017–2018 channel-inventory and crypto-demand statements; damages remain unspecified and NVIDIA continues to defend the case. It is a governance reminder, not a quantified valuation deduction.

Three-year operating and owner-cash framework

Owner free cash flow to the firm, or owner FCFF, treats SBC as an economic expense: it starts with after-tax operating profit, adds depreciation, then deducts capex and incremental working-capital absorption without adding SBC back. This is stricter than conventional cash FCF and makes extended customer terms explicit.

RISKS · MONITORING

The operating thesis is intact; the evidentiary burden is higher

Strongest evidence for

Data Center, cloud demand and architecture cadence remain exceptional. Rubin is already shipping, and the outlook excludes a China recovery.

Strongest evidence against

Part of future demand depends on NVIDIA investments, extended terms, cloud purchases, leases or guarantees whose returns and loss probabilities are undisclosed.

Alternative explanation

Goldman's post-call view is that clearer margin and commitment disclosure improves risk assessment. It does not resolve cash timing, contract overlap or loss probability.

Resolution path

Track sell-through, utilization, receivable aging, commitment reduction, realized guarantee cash, Rubin mix and normalized gross margin together.

FINAL JUDGMENT

Constructive, but no longer a pure platform multiple

NVIDIA still owns the strongest operating evidence in AI infrastructure. The new discount rate belongs to credit allocation, not to demand.

At the post-results indication, the Base framework spans US$254–321 per share. The Bear range is US$106–148, while the Bull methods converge near US$538–551. The asymmetry supports a constructive long-term view, but not a low-risk earnings chase: only the lower Base endpoint offers a modest margin of safety, and the Bear downside is large.

Stance: positive with a higher risk classification. The thesis strengthens if the next-quarter guide converts into Rubin mix, cash and stable receivable quality while scheduled commitments decline through use. It weakens if the disclosed margin trough is breached or contingent support begins turning into assumed leases and cash payments.

Strongest counterargument: management's preliminary FY2028 growth outlook is supply-constrained, and the balance-sheet structures may create a second revenue stream with limited realized loss. Failure boundary: customer-financed demand, weaker cash conversion and lower margins persisting together would invalidate the premium platform valuation. Confidence is medium because the operating disclosures are strong but customer-level utilization, returns and credit economics are absent.
