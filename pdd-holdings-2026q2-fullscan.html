THE THIRTY-SECOND VERSION

A low earnings multiple matters only if profits are sustainable

  • Who: PDD owns Pinduoduo and Temu. Merchants pay for visibility and transaction-related services; the Nasdaq security is an ADS in the Cayman holding company.
  • This period: Q2 revenue reached RMB112.4bn, up 8.1%; operating profit improved, but attributable net income fell 11.6%.
  • The tension: Strong consolidated cash generation can fund the strategy. Separate returns from domestic commerce, Temu and new initiatives remain unreported.
  • The trap: Cash plus short-term investments is not a promised shareholder payout. Also, transaction-services growth is not a disclosed Temu growth rate.
  • Verdict: Neutral (watch). Price $82.21; primary present values $50.42 Bear and $97.38 Base. The next results must show whether fulfillment spending preserves operating economics.
PRIMER

Follow the merchant payment, then the claim on the cash

PDD is principally a marketplace operator, not a conventional retailer. Merchants offer goods to shoppers; PDD earns online-marketing fees for exposure and transaction-related fees, including fulfillment services. Consumer purchases, merchant revenue and PDD revenue are different amounts. A higher fee line can reflect changing service mix as well as greater merchandise activity.

Step 1
Shoppers place orders

Demand brings merchants to Pinduoduo and Temu.

Step 2
Merchants pay for services

Advertising buys visibility; transaction-related services support selling and fulfillment.

Step 3
The platform pays its costs

Traffic, technology, staff, support and delivery arrangements absorb part of the fees.

Step 4
Cash is retained or deployed

Merchant settlements are obligations; retained corporate funds are not automatically distributions.

An American Depositary Share (ADS) represents an interest in the Cayman parent, which owns subsidiaries and consolidates certain mainland operations through variable interest entity (VIE) contracts. It does not give an investor direct equity ownership in the VIE. The group reports a single e-commerce reportable segment; online marketing and transaction services are revenue types, not separate Pinduoduo and Temu accounts.

Platforms can lower merchant charges, invest in delivery or sell more services per order. These choices can lift activity before profit. Customs and safety rules also change cross-border versus local fulfillment costs. The analysis separates those business choices from assumptions about their future returns.

EARNINGS QUALITY

The unidentified loss prevents a clean earnings normalization

The operating business improved, but the full income statement did not. The profit bridge isolates why the bottom line fell.

Sales and marketing increased 9.0%, general and administrative expense 52.9%, and R&D 27.2%. These increases are real group costs. Allocating them among Temu, domestic merchant support, first-party brands and corporate overhead would require disclosure the company has not provided. Consumer incentives can reduce recognized revenue or enter marketing expense depending on their contractual form, so a fee-growth comparison alone cannot reconstruct the subsidy budget.

REINVESTMENT

The strategy needs a return bridge, not a larger ambition

PDD’s reinvestment needs an observable return bridge. Order growth, broader services and higher fees can each expand revenue, but have different costs.

Transaction services contributed 76.9% of the Q2 revenue increase. That concentration makes its cost structure consequential. Marketing’s slower expansion could reflect support policies, merchant demand, category mix or other factors; the available data do not isolate the cause. A durable competitive advantage would show up in merchants retaining attractive economics while platform earnings and owner cash grow per ADS, not simply in consolidated scale.

Management’s ambition to build ‘another PDD’ within 3 years is an aspiration without a defined revenue, earnings or valuation baseline. The RMB100bn support-program label is separate: it is not a disclosed cash-spending total, booked expense or first-party-brand budget. The first-party brands initiative is an execution strategy involving manufacturers, product development and quality standards. Its name does not establish who owns inventory or whether future revenue will be reported gross or net.

EU order exposure: partial answer

Asked about order impact, management described higher cost and lower fulfillment efficiency for affected cross-border markets, then local merchant and fulfillment expansion. It gave no group exposure percentage.

Brand rollout: partial answer

Management said initial progress was slower than expected and described selective manufacturer collaboration. No named launch schedule, financial outcome or revised timetable was supplied.

Warehouses: direction answered

The response identified domestic rural delivery and overseas transit consolidation priorities. It did not quantify budgets or returns; ownership was a diligence gap, not a separately asked question.

Merchant advertising: unanswered

Asked whether merchants were more willing to advertise, management returned to ecosystem health. That language does not establish a recovery in advertising demand.

The call also described first-party and third-party products as complementary, but supplied no mix targets or safeguards against channel conflict. Inventory ownership, price setting, launch economics and allocation of promotional support are the disclosures that would make this claim testable. A platform that both sets marketplace rules and supports selected brands may face conflicting incentives even without holding inventory itself; the current record establishes the strategic tension, not abusive conduct.

Localization has a plausible economic rationale: nearby inventory, local merchants and more reliable fulfillment can reduce exposure to individual cross-border parcel routes. But the benefit is not automatic. Building a network before sufficient order density arrives can absorb cash and lower margins. Management described targeted development and operation of overseas transit warehouses, not an asset-free transition. The disclosed group investment increase cannot be allocated to this initiative without a spending bridge.

Quick commerce provides a separate competitive question. Management described different use cases and limited synergies with the existing model, and emphasized its current strengths. That explains a strategic priority, not proof that consumer mindshare is unaffected. The appropriate test is whether the core merchant proposition retains demand and engagement while rivals pursue frequency; there is no basis here for a market-share forecast.

Call evidence: August 24 interpreted transcript; original Chinese prevails. Finance figures are controlled by the SEC exhibit. The transcript contains an apparent first-party/third-party wording error and an incorrect cash figure; neither is imported. Unasked subjects are recorded as disclosure gaps, not evasive answers.

PLAIN ENGLISH

A service line can grow while its return deteriorates, or while it funds a valuable new network. The report cannot distinguish those outcomes by assigning group fees to Temu. What would resolve it is a consistent contribution-profit or local-fulfillment operating measure, supported by the group margin and cash record.

Restricted cash mainly comprises consumer funds in supervised accounts reserved for merchant payment. Excluding that asset does not eliminate all merchant obligations: the payable and deposit balances also matter to operating liquidity. Conversely, deducting every operating liability as financial debt would ignore their business funding cycle. This report therefore shows the obligations and applies an explicit reserve/access stress to the narrow pool; it does not claim to have reconstructed entity-level surplus cash.

Other non-current assets mainly comprise time deposits and debt securities, not exclusively financial assets. Excluding the whole line is conservative, not an impairment finding; verified composition, maturity and offshore availability could justify future credit. Restricted mainland net assets are a different legal measure, addressed in governance.

Settlement timing, tax and working capital affect operating cash flow. Interim investing flows include portfolio activity, while net-property movements include depreciation; neither identifies current capex or a warehouse budget. Annual FCF is a historical reference, not proof that the new strategy remains equally capital-light.

The group’s capital-return policy remains retention for long-term business development. The annual report states no present foreseeable cash-dividend plan, and no new issuer equity-repurchase program was found in the bounded post-period update. Repayment or repurchase of convertible notes is debt repayment, not an equity buyback. A director selling personal ADSs is also not a company distribution. Consequently the one-year model assumes no cash dividend or buyback carry; this is a disclosed-policy-based scenario, not a permanent prohibition on future returns.

The five disclosed 2018–2020 equity offerings raised approximately US$8.6bn in total net proceeds, calculated from rounded offering amounts; convertible-note issuance is excluded. The company had declared or paid no cash dividends through the April 29, 2026 annual filing. This is evidence of capital raised versus cash dividends returned, not a reconstruction of all historical repurchases. Retention can still create value if incremental returns are attractive, but balances alone do not demonstrate that return.

PLAIN ENGLISH

The RMB451.6bn liquidity measure is not money already in investors’ pockets. A large asset balance alone cannot show how much shareholders could receive. Investment-maturity details and a capital-return policy would clarify that question.

The holding company’s ability to distribute money partly depends on subsidiaries paying dividends and on contractual fees from VIE entities. PRC statutory reserves, entity earnings and currency conversion rules can limit those flows. The restricted-net-assets figure combines paid-in capital, reserves and VIE net assets; it is neither a cash-location schedule nor a quantified probability of permanent loss. The valuation’s cash credit reflects uncertainty about reserves and access once, rather than subtracting this figure again.

Related-party payment arrangements create governance questions even when economically necessary. The annual filing describes interest-free loans to an executive-controlled payment company and related settlement receivables. The relevant tests are repayment terms, collateral if any, settlement aging, commercial alternatives and independent oversight. Calling the whole balance impaired or labeling every related-party transaction an extraction would go beyond the evidence. Equally, a stated commercial rationale does not establish arm’s-length pricing.

Stock compensation should be assessed in both the income statement and the share register. A lower expense can reflect grant timing, vesting and valuation, while a lower diluted weighted average can reflect option arithmetic. Neither proves an equivalent fall in future grants. Legal issued shares include escrowed shares excluded from EPS, so the long-run legal-share increase cannot be treated as a one-for-one realized dilution rate.

At December 2025, outstanding options covered 355.2m ordinary shares and restricted share units (RSUs), employee share awards, 55.6m. Their gross sum was 410.8m, or 7.2% of legal shares—not all incremental EPS dilution. Options’ weighted exercise price was US$0.0065 per ordinary share. Unrecognized compensation was RMB12.81bn for options and RMB3.60bn for RSUs, expected over weighted-average periods of 1.93 and 1.84 years. Forfeitures and future grants can change expense.

Options vest 25% annually over 4 explicit service years; vested shares have a 3-year transfer restriction unless prior written consent is obtained. Repurchase on termination during that restriction is discretionary. RSUs vest 25% annually or 50% in year 2 then 25% in years 3 and 4. Public descriptions give no quantified financial-performance hurdles; individual agreements can differ. The up-to-3% annual pool increase is authorization, not an actual grant rate. The next award reconciliation should identify grants, vesting, cancellations and outstanding claims; alignment with per-share returns remains less clear.

At March 18, 2026, Huang, Tencent and PDD Partnership held 24.8%, 13.8% and 6.5%, respectively. Huang directs the partnership’s general partner and holding vehicle, so these blocks must not be summed into an unsupported control percentage. The company reported only Class A shares; authorized dual-class provisions were not live super-voting stock. Large blocks can still influence governance without establishing unilateral control.

The key incentive test is whether future disclosures reward per-ADS returns rather than continued service or business expansion alone.

PLAIN ENGLISH

Employee share awards can reduce each investor’s share of future value. Separately, contractual rights affect how cash moves within the group. The next annual filing should reconcile shares and awards issued, vested, cancelled and outstanding, and clarify transfers from operating entities to the parent.

Base present value is $97.38, Bear $50.42, against $82.21. The chosen earnings multiple matters most; operating margin is second. Evidence that local fulfillment preserves profit, or that cash can reach shareholders, would strengthen the favorable case.

VALUATION APPENDIX

Cash is added once, and every outcome uses one horizon

Normalized operating net profit after tax (NOPAT) = forward revenue × SBC-inclusive margin × (1 − tax). Owner FCF = NOPAT × assumed conversion. These scenario measures exclude portfolio income and unidentified other items; they are not reported interim cash flow.

Recognized ending cash = credit × (June narrow liquidity + forward owner FCF), with no additional interest income. Terminal equity wealth = NOPAT × P/E + recognized cash, converted by FX and ADS. Discount it one year for the tiles’ present values. Retained cash enters share value once; cash distributions remain zero.

The share conversion first divides the reported diluted ordinary-share count by the ADS ratio. It is a weighted-average proxy; the release provides no exact June-end diluted count. The cash proxy starts from cash and short investments less leases, excludes restricted cash and the full other-non-current-asset line, and is not increased for cash generated between June and the September market date. This timing convention uses the latest published balance; it does not claim a verified intervening cash movement.

The weighted diluted ADS proxy and FX are held constant throughout the forecast. Future grants, share-price-dependent option dilution and currency movements are not separately projected. SBC remains in operating costs, but this does not mean it was cash-paid or offset by buybacks. The owner-cash conversion is a conservative valuation reserve, not a forecast of the exact GAAP cash balance.

The DCF cross-check capitalizes forward owner FCF as a growing perpetuity, then adds recognized initial narrow cash. It does not add the first forward cash flow again as a separate terminal asset. Its steady-growth assumptions are 0% / 2% / 3%, based on a fade below the initial revenue-growth cases rather than management guidance. The primary P/E diagnostic solves sustainable growth from cash conversion, multiple and required return. Those mechanically implied rates are a consistency test, not reported forecasts.
